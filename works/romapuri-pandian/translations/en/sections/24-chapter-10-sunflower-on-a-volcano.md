10

Sunflower on a Volcano

<!-- source boundary: scan 143 → scan 144 -->

Thamarai hurried to the place where Sezhiyan was imprisoned. She knocked on the door. There was no reply from inside. After telling the warriors to stay alert, she opened the door; it was pitch-dark within. Was this a royal prison? No. The very place where the king lived looked like a prison. What, then, must the prison be like!

Thamarai had a torch brought, took it in her hand and went inside step by step, looking all around. That cruel prison, dug into the earth, was full of mud and slush. In that cave-like structure, one had to walk some distance before reaching the place where the prisoner was kept. How much worse might that place be? Something scurried across her foot. She lowered the torch and looked. A large rat darted toward the door. After that, she was afraid even to walk on.

She also grew angry with her brother Irungovel. Whatever the circumstances, enemies should not be treated with such cruelty, she muttered to herself. To lock up a good warrior in a place unfit even for buffaloes was utterly inhuman, she thought bitterly.

She stopped for a moment, calmed herself and held the light forward. Sezhiyan was there. He had not escaped anywhere. That reassured her! Was he alive? She looked closely. He was breathing. But he lay unconscious. She touched him; his forehead and feet had not gone cold. There was warmth in him. Calling to the warriors who had followed her, she ordered, "Run and quickly bring water and gruel."

<!-- source boundary: scan 144 → scan 145 -->

She sat beside him and gently moved his face, trying to rouse him. Apart from the fact that he was not dead, the sight of him lying there was like that of a corpse laid out. Thinking of how his parents would suffer if they saw this cruelty, she shed tears.

A warrior brought water and gruel. She sprinkled water on his face, lifted his head and slowly propped him against the wall. She took the gruel and held it near his mouth. He could not even open his mouth. She pressed the bowl to his lips and insisted, "Drink a little! Just a little, a little!" until she made him drink. Without being conscious of it, Sezhiyan drank. She could hear the sound of the gruel falling into his stomach.

After a long time Sezhiyan opened his eyes. At the sight of light in the dark cave, his eyes lit up; without realizing it he cried, "Light! Light! Light!" He came fully awake with a start. Opening his eyes wide, he looked all around. Was what he was seeing a dream?—the question leapt up. He rubbed his eyes and looked again. He had not gone anywhere. He was still here, in the enemy's heavily guarded prison-fortress. There was no confusion about it. No doubt. It was certain truth! A clear reality! With a deep sigh he looked at Thamarai. So many meanings lay hidden in his gaze. She understood only one: it seemed as though he were looking at her and asking, 'You are torturing me like this; is it right?'

"How do you feel now?" Thamarai asked in a voice filled with compassion.

"Very comfortable. Ah! What a wonderfully cool prison! A floor as though sandal paste had been poured over it; windows just right for a gentle breeze; the fragrance of jasmine piercing the nose—what could I possibly lack here? Even the man who wrote stories about a hell-world could not have created a place like this with all his powers of imagination!" Sezhiyan replied sarcastically.

<!-- source boundary: scan 145 → scan 146 -->

Thamarai could not bear to listen to words that seemed to belittle her brother's greatness. Yet she remained silent, deciding that she should not answer in a way that would wound a man already lying there exhausted.

Sezhiyan, however, seemed to want to speak with her further. "I want to talk with you alone for a little while. May I?" he asked with a certain air.

Once before, she had managed the situation without allowing him to speak to her. But now she could not openly ignore Sezhiyan's request.

After looking Sezhiyan up and down once, Thamarai signalled to the warriors to go and stand at the doorway. They planted the torch upright in the mud there, moved toward the entrance and stood guard cautiously.

"Hm! What do you want to talk about? Speak quickly!" said Thamarai.

"I am not going to be the only one giving a speech. You too must give me a few explanations in answer to my questions," said Sezhiyan.

"Very nice indeed! Are we supposed to give explanations about ourselves to a warrior from an enemy country and let ourselves be deceived?"

"I do not need news of that kind. Even if truths like that came into my hands, how could I get out of here? You people would not even throw my corpse outside! Suppose you did throw my corpse out, and some dog, jackal or vulture tore at my body, carried away my heart and dropped it somewhere in the Pandiyan country or the Chola country—what would you do if the truth that my heart belongs to the Pandiyan-Chola lands were thereby established? Or suppose a crow accidentally carried away my tongue and dropped it before the Cholas or the Pandiyans, and my tongue immediately began speaking like the tongue in a legend and told them the truths about you—could that not happen?"

<!-- source boundary: scan 146 → scan 147 -->

"That is why you will burn even my corpse right here and dispose of it. Before that, I thought I might learn a few truths and enjoy a little temporary happiness. Surely there is no obstacle to my attaining at least this small happiness?"

As Thamarai listened to him, her heart began to melt. She was amazed to see a light shining on the face of a man who seemed certain of death, without even the faintest shadow of fear.

"What is it that you want to ask me about?" she asked in a voice softened by affection.

"First let me tell you a story about myself," he began. "One day my king, Peruvazhuthi Pandiyan, called me and showed me a portrait of a beautiful woman. 'She is the woman who is going to become your wife,' he said. I looked at the portrait and raised no objection. He understood my consent. Later, because of the political circumstances that arose, the marriage did not take place. Ever since then I have kept searching for the beauty I saw in that portrait, but I have not found her."

"So what? Did you make me sit here just to hear this story?"

"Wait a little! It was while I was searching that the Chola-Pandiyan friendship agreement took place. What happened during that celebration brought me to this fate."

"I already know all that. So what?"

"At the time when I wanted to live—to live prosperously—to live happily—and lay longing for the day marriage would come, that painted maiden never appeared before my eyes. When I built thousands upon thousands of castles in my mind, dreaming of speaking words of love with her and dancing the joyful dance of married life, that talking dove never fell into the net of my eyes.

My life—my golden lamp—beauty beyond anything on this earth—pure gold without the slightest alloy! I wrote line after line praising her in such words, stacked up those palm-leaf manuscripts, lifted them onto my head, and said, 'All of this is an epic about my beloved

<!-- source boundary: scan 147 → scan 148 -->

woman,' dreaming that I would dance with joy and make a home with her. Yet I could not find her then. Now, when death waves its hand and calls, 'Come! Come!' I see my sandalwood statue on the road to death. And I weep!"

"You saw her? Where?... Who is she...!"

"Yes, I saw her. A face like a red lotus freshly in bloom. A body of pure gold. A colored statue in which all beauty has been moulded together. People may call her eyes fish—no; I would call them lily flowers in which honey-drinking beetles lie. People may call her lips petals—no; I would call them an open vermilion casket! I have seen that celestial maiden—I have seen that portrait of beauty!"

"Where? Whom?" Thamarai asked angrily.

"Right here! You! You are the blue lotus I have wandered searching for! Pearl! Moon!"

Thamarai was jolted with shock.

She seemed to turn into a statue on the spot. She did not know what answer to give him. From the beginning she had decided only that she should not speak in a way that would hurt him. She was not angry at the thought he held about her. He had a right to marvel at her beauty and to feel drawn to her. How could she prevent that? There was nothing wrong in a man giving his heart to a woman at first sight. Nor was there anything wrong in expressing his feelings to her. The question was how far such a situation could go... how far it could rightly go. There was nothing wrong so long as he merely asked, 'I love you more than life itself; accept me!' Certainly nothing wrong. And if the maiden who heard those words gave her consent, there was nothing more to say.

But if, after she rejected him because she did not love him, he still tried to behave wrongly toward her, that was where a man became a beast. Such a beast must be punished.

A thought like this made Thamarai feel sympathy for him. Before hostility arose between the Chola and Irungovel, the Pandiyan and Irungovel too had been friends.

At that time Irungovel might have sent her portrait to the Pandiyan king, and Sezhiyan might have fallen in love with her on seeing it. From the way he spoke, that love seemed pure. At the same time, fear arose too. Could Sezhiyan not have planned to deceive her with a love story and escape?

Having decided not to speak in a way that would cut him down, she said, "If you learn who I am, you will change your mind."

Sezhiyan did not reply. He waited eagerly to hear what she was going to say.

"I am King Irungovel's younger sister," she said proudly. Hearing those words, Sezhiyan was shaken.

"How can a sunflower bloom on a volcano?" he asked himself.

If he had replied, "What does it matter who you are? Love does not look at enmity!"

Thamarai would have concluded that he had devised this plan and was acting out a love drama only to deceive her.

But when she saw his anguish on hearing that she was the enemy's sister, she understood that he truly loved her.

"Did you not hear what I said? This Thamarai is King Irungovel's younger sister," she repeated emphatically.

"Why were you born into this family!" Sezhiyan cried out as pain burst from him.

<!-- source boundary: scan 149 → scan 150 -->

His cry startled her. Hearing some sound near the prison door, she turned around. A warrior came running and said, "The king is coming." Thamarai slowly moved forward, still looking toward the entrance. Irungovel was coming quickly inside, stepping through the mud and slush.

Wanting to speak first before her brother could ask anything, Thamarai began, "Brother! This prisoner suddenly had a tightness in his chest and was in danger, brother!"

Looking angrily at his sister, Irungovel mocked her: "Oh! The prisoner was in danger, so the guards came running at once. You felt compassion for this prisoner and flew here to treat him. Is that it?" Realizing the anger seething beneath her brother's mocking words, Thamarai stood with her head bowed. Irungovel glared at Sezhiyan.

"Why, young man? Did you expect a prison to be like the sleeping chamber in your own house? Why trouble everyone else over it? It seems you cannot even spend the time until you die with some dignity!"

Sezhiyan, who had remained quiet until then, looked at Irungovel with utter contempt and replied, "I did not call anyone here. If you want to kill me, finish that work quickly. Do not instead bind my hands and feet and keep provoking my heart!" Irungovel's anger flared.

"What did you say?" he cried and lunged at Sezhiyan. But before he could reach him, Thamarai ran between them and stopped her brother. "Even if he is from an enemy country, he is still a warrior. To treat him like this brings disgrace upon the code of our warrior clan, brother!" she blurted out in a rush. Only after she had finished speaking did fear seize her. She had said far too much in front of her brother.

"Thamarai! No one called you here to explain our code of conduct. Go outside!" Irungovel shouted.
