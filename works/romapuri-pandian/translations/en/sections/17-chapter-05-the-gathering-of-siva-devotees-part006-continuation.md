Her inner struggle began again. Did the Yavana elder know who she really was? What would he do if he understood that it was Muthunagai who was roaming about in men's clothes? Questions like these unsettled her mind. Inside the temple, the Yavana elder had seen her only as Muthunagai. There was no way he could have seen her put on the male disguise again. She consoled herself with that thought.

Why should Irungovel, in the guise of the Yavana elder, have saved her? Why did he have such affection for her?

There was no answer.

After seeing the conduct of that Thiruneettradiyar, she found herself developing a hatred for the whole group!

What more shameful example could there be of the honesty and discipline of men who had renounced desire for land, gold and women? She was deeply agitated. By then she had reached the entrance of her house.

She stopped the horse and looked toward the house. A horse was tied at the entrance. She dismounted quietly, went to the doorway and looked inside. Her father and that Yavana elder were talking about something in great secrecy. She could see the two men talking, but could not hear what they were saying. Muthunagai felt a surge of fury. She decided at once to go to the palace, tell Karikalan, bring soldiers and have the Yavana elder arrested. In her anger she did not know what she was doing. It did not occur to her that if Irungovel were seized from her own house, her father too would be exposed! She had no time to think; all that swayed within her heart was pride in the Chola land and devotion to it.

The horse sped toward the palace. Muthunagai entered Karikalan's residence. She was desperate to see the king at once. "The king has gone out to wander alone. It is not possible now!" came the reply from the bodyguard.

"Can the king be allowed to go alone with no one accompanying him?" Muthunagai asked anxiously.

The bodyguard replied, "No one can defeat Karikalan by confronting him alone. No one needs to worry about that!"

At once Muthunagai said in alarm, "This is the moment to catch Irungovel red-handed; fifty soldiers must be sent with me!"

The bodyguard thought for a moment and hesitated. "But I cannot send them without the king's orders!"

"The king has already said that I am to be given whatever I need. And what I am asking for now is not for myself, is it? It is to capture an enemy of this country!" she explained, insisting on her request.

The bodyguard sent fifty of the soldiers under his command to follow Muthunagai. Taking them with her, she headed toward her house with the exultation of one who felt that Irungovel was already as good as caught.

Seeing fifty palace soldiers suddenly moving at great speed through the street, the people of the capital were startled. Everyone inside the houses rushed out into the street. Muthunagai, riding at the front, pulled hard on the reins and stopped her horse. What a bitter disappointment?

The Yavana elder's horse, which had been tied at the entrance of her house, was gone. She dismounted and hurried toward the house; it was locked.

"We were too late; the traitor has escaped!" Muthunagai muttered. Wearily, all the horses turned back toward the palace.

Muthunagai wrote on a palm leaf, "Delay; because of it he escaped," gave it to a soldier with instructions to hand it to the bodyguard, and continued her journey in another direction.

After Peruvazhuthi Pandiyan expressed his wish to return to the capital, the Chola king <!-- source boundary: scan 87 → scan 88 -->went to the Pandiyan's residence. The Pandiyan spoke openly to the Chola king, saying that apart from Sezhiyan's disappearance, he had no other worry.

Karikalan reassured Peruvazhuthi that "responsible men have been appointed to gather information about Sezhiyan and about the conspiratorial schemes being carried out to overthrow the Chola realm," and that "there is no need for any concern about Sezhiyan." They then spoke at length about the relations between the Pandiyan country and the Chola land and about agreements of friendship. Both men praised the poet Karikannanar, who had shown the way toward their unity and friendship between the two countries.

"Karikannanar has a daughter. Her name is Muthunagai. I intend to conduct her marriage with all the splendour of the wedding of a princess of this country," said Karikalan.

"Is that so? I am very happy. But one thing: I ask that you not begin the wedding arrangements without involving me," said Peruvazhuthi.

Karikalan smiled and replied, "Very well, then!"

Karikalan turned back only after the Pandiyan's party and retinue had gone a long distance and disappeared from sight. To his astonishment, a soldier came riding at great speed, saluted Karikalan and stood before him.

Before Karikalan could finish asking, "Who are you?" the soldier took out the rolled palm leaf in his hand and held it out. The king eagerly took it and read.

"This is what I write to Chola king Karikalan: the Pandiyan warrior Sezhiyan lies enslaved in my custody. If you wish to hand him over alive to the Pandiyan, you must return my country to me. Otherwise I intend to torture Sezhiyan and kill him little by little! Answer at once—through action!

— King Irungovel."

<!-- source boundary: scan 88 → scan 89 -->

After reading the letter, Karikalan looked up. The man who had brought it was nowhere to be seen. He had vanished in a flash.

Karikalan's eyes reddened. He ground his teeth. His hand tightened around the hilt of the sword he held. He strode back to his palace like a lion. "Where are the commanders?" he roared. Within an instant, the commanders had gathered before him.

"The war drums of our armies must sound in every region around the Chola land—yes, even within the Chola land itself. Wherever this Irungovel may be, he must be seized and brought here at once!" thundered the glory of the Cholas.

The commanders obeyed the urgent order, drew their valiant swords and saluted the king. Just then the poet Karikannanar arrived there in great haste.

"Wait, my king! Wait! Calm yourself! Calm yourself!" cried the poet, raising his hand as he came and stood before him. Karikalan looked at him. "Poet! What are you saying?" he asked in agitation.

"Capturing and killing Irungovel is no great task for us! But is not our aim to rescue Sezhiyan alive?" asked the poet.

"Here! Read that threatening palm leaf from Irungovel. No one has ever threatened Karikalan until now. This coward, who was defeated by me and ran away to hide, is threatening me. Am I to cower in fear of him and die? Never, never. The valour of the Chola land has not withered away. The warriors of our land are accustomed to grasping swords, not the feet of their enemies. We are a lineage that has raised the cry of battle beneath the shadow of the tiger flag. We take pride in receiving honourable battle wounds; we do not fall at an enemy's feet. No one is to stop Karikalan. Within a day or two, the head of that madman Irungovel must bow before me. Until then I have no other thought."

With that roar, Karikalan set out.

<!-- source boundary: scan 89 → scan 90 -->

The poet approached him with a smile. "Great Chola king! What promise did you make to Peruvazhuthi Pandiyan? That you would somehow rescue Sezhiyan and return him, did you not? Can that be forgotten? Do you think that just as Irungovel wrote to you, he did not also send a letter to the Pandiyan king? Peruvazhuthi too has received such a letter!"

"Is that so?" Karikalan was utterly astonished.

"Yes. Why do you think Peruvazhuthi Pandiyan suddenly set out for the capital?"

"What is the reason, poet?"

"Irungovel has written to him too. In other words, this entire arrangement is meant to damage the friendship between the Pandiyan country and the Chola country!"

"How, poet?"

"Is it not clear? Irungovel's warning is this: if the lands that were taken from him are returned, he will give Sezhiyan back; otherwise he will kill Sezhiyan!"

"Yes!"

"The Pandiyan may think, 'Why not return the lands and rescue Sezhiyan?' If you refuse to do so, the Pandiyan may become hostile toward you. Irungovel's aim may be to kindle that hostility and make his conspiracy succeed. Unable to deal with such a difficult situation, the Pandiyan king has left for the capital immediately!"

"All right... then what should we do now?"

"A plot must be defeated by a plot!"

"Should we return his lands according to Irungovel's condition?"

"That is not what I said, my king! If you send soldiers in every direction and provoke him, he may suddenly kill Sezhiyan!"

"Then shall we ask the Pandiyan king for his opinion on this?"

"He himself left because he could make no sense of it and did not wish to trouble you!"

"Poet! This palm leaf is a challenge thrown at the valour of our country! I must accept it. Irungovel will not get his land back, and he will not be able to kill Sezhiyan either," Karikalan declared defiantly.

Just then a soldier entered the palace. Karikalan stared in surprise, unable to recognize him. The nearby bodyguard whispered into Karikalan's ear, "The one coming in is Muthunagai!" Karikalan glanced once at the poet and remained alert. The poet did not recognize that the person approaching him was his own daughter.

Muthunagai signalled to Karikalan with her expression that she wanted to speak in secret. He looked at the poet and the others and said, "All right; I need to speak with the spy... everyone may leave. If necessary, I will call you again." He followed the poet for seven paces, saw him off, and then went into the inner part of the palace with Muthunagai. Her troubled eyes proclaimed that she was thinking about how she had behaved before her own father as though she did not know him.

Karikalan looked at her and said, "You really are very clever! I could not recognize you at all!" He marvelled at her skill.

"You doubted me at first, didn't you? See, I managed to bewilder even you for a moment!" said Muthunagai with a laugh.

"That is all well and good, but your voice gives you away!" said the king.

"That is why I am playing the mute! I say that I am observing a vow of silence and converse only through my stylus!"

<!-- source boundary: scan 91 → scan 92 -->

"Very well! How successful have your efforts been? Can you tell me anything?"

Muthunagai looked all around her.

"Do not be afraid! There is no one here. Speak boldly!"

"I have more or less discovered where Irungovel is staying!"

"How? How?"

"Because of this disguise, something strange happened. On the first day I wandered all through the forests and uplands and stopped on the bank of a waterfall; then a woman came. She fell in love with me!"

"What? She fell in love with you? Mad woman! Who is she?"

"Her name is Thamarai?"

"Thamarai! That is a beautiful name!"

"On the first day I failed to learn which pond that lotus had bloomed in. By then she had left. At her request, I went there again the next day!"

"Oh? And she came too, I suppose?"

"Would she stay away? She came all alone. On the very first day I had told that golden-skinned woman that, for the sake of my ideal, I was observing a vow not to speak! Following the same plan, I began my mute act again the next day. She told me, weeping, that from the moment she saw me she had lost her heart and had been pining in longing!"

"Hm... the usual opening ceremony of love! Then what?"

"What else...? I had become trapped in a tangle I could not escape. When I saw the force of her love, I wondered whether I should simply tell her, 'My dear! I too am a woman like you!' But then, poor thing! I was afraid her heart would burst, so I said nothing!"

"Leave that aside! Who is she, girl?"

"Is that not the most important thing? Only after finding that out did I assure her that I too loved her!"

<!-- source boundary: scan 92 → scan 93 -->

"Then?"

"My king! She is our enemy's younger sister! Yes—Irungovel's own sister!"

"Ah! Is that so?"

"Somehow, a way has opened for me to enter the enemy's hiding place. She parted from me saying that she would meet me again tomorrow. She also said that she would tell some lie to her elder brother, trick him somehow, and take me with her."

"What will you do, Muthunagai, if Irungovel's sister is just as cunning as he is and deceives you?"

"There is no need for fear—I will deceive all of them and succeed. Whoever tries to dig a pit beneath the greatness of the sacred Chola land, I will stake my life and try to destroy him. This resolve will protect me!"

"Muthunagai! Look at this threatening letter Irungovel has written!" said the Chola king, handing her the palm leaf. She read it and then gave it back to the king.

"A stratagem must be defeated by a stratagem. If we attack with troops, it may become difficult to recover Sezhiyan alive. Whatever happens, please wait until I come and meet you next time." Saying this, Muthunagai took leave of the king, mounted her horse and rode away.
