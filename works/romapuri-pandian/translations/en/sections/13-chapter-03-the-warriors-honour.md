3

The Warrior's Honour

<!-- source boundary: scan 57 → scan 58 -->

Far from the city of Puhar, the band of devotees moved swiftly into a forest path. Unable to keep carrying the *anna-kavadi* in which Sezhiyan had been bound, they had taken turns bearing it until all their shoulders were swollen. No sooner had they entered the forest path than everyone sat down to rest.

Sezhiyan, inside the *anna-kavadi*, began to stir as he came out of his stupor. Seeing the movement, the devotees grew alarmed. They drew their swords and stood ready. Two of them went near, lifted Sezhiyan out from where he lay cramped inside the *anna-kavadi*, and immediately two others fastened the shackles they had kept ready onto his hands and feet. Sezhiyan woke without understanding anything. He stared at the devotees. Their fear had now left them. Emboldened by the thought that they had come far away from the Chola capital, they threw off their disguises. Sezhiyan understood clearly now that they were Irungovel's men.

What could he do? With his hands and feet bound, he cast his eyes as far as they would reach and let his thoughts wander still farther. However far a kite may fly when released into the sky, does it not finally return to the place where the end of its string is held? In the same way, his thoughts, after flying everywhere, finally circled back within himself.

He decided that the one thing that had to happen immediately was his escape from Irungovel's warriors. But how was he to carry out that decision? Darkness everywhere! That too was a circumstance in his favour. If he ran, no one could easily chase and follow him. But first he had to escape! What was he to do for that? He thought he might try talking to those warriors with their frenzied faces. He began to speak.

<!-- source boundary: scan 58 → scan 59 -->

"Who are you?" That was his first question.

"Don't you know us? A band that avenges itself upon its enemies!" came the proud reply.

"Where are you taking me now?"

"Taking you? You fool! We are not taking you along. We are dragging you—to King Irungovel!"

"Irungovel! Is he still a king? Is he even alive? I heard that, ashamed of having shown his back in battle, he killed himself!"

"What arrogance this fellow has!" one warrior roared.

"Tear his mouth apart!" another shouted.

"Cut out his tongue and throw it away!" cried a warrior, rushing forward with a knife.

None of those cries caused even the slightest fear in Sezhiyan. The more they shouted in fury, the more he stood erect like a lion. He was a great warrior born in the Maravar tradition of men who, when the war drum sounds, charge their enemies without even noticing a *kaarthai* that has torn loose and fallen. The sight of him standing mountain-like with his chest thrust high made the warriors surrounding him stare in astonishment.

One of them came close to Sezhiyan and said, "Young man! You may be a great warrior in the Pandiyan country—like iluppai blossom passing for sugar in a town with no mill! Don't wag your tail here! We of the Velir clan have lost only our country to Karikala Cholan; we have not yet lost our valour, our honour, or our power to finish a bitter feud. Nor are we going to leave him unavenged. The matter should have been finished today itself. You interfered and ruined it..." He let the words fall with great pride.

Sezhiyan looked at him and growled, "Oh! From whom did you learn the art of circling around an enemy from outside? Who sold you that skill that does not belong to this land? You would kill, before my very eyes, Karikala Peruvalathan, the treasure of the great Chola country. Was I supposed simply to stand and watch? Then why should I have a spear, shield and dagger in my hands?"

<!-- source boundary: scan 59 → scan 60 -->

"Why talk to him? Drag him along!" one man howled in a harsh voice. Another rushed forward to spit on Sezhiyan. A third caught hold of him and pulled him aside.

They dragged Sezhiyan along the forest path with shackles on his hands and feet. It was a frenzy. Was it a dance of joy because an enemy had been captured? The group entered the heart of the forest. At the noise of their dancing and singing, even the wild animals ran and hid. Birds, losing their calm, beat their wings up from the branches and settled again. Suddenly the clamour of the group stopped. Everyone started and looked ahead. Tearing through the darkness, the light of a torch was coming rapidly toward them. Sezhiyan and the warriors stood motionless.

The approaching light came to a halt a short distance in front of them. All Sezhiyan could make out was a warrior seated on a horse holding a torch. Who was he? From what country was he? Sezhiyan could not tell.

The warrior with the torch called out three times, "Enemy! Enemy! Enemy!"

At once the warriors who had brought Sezhiyan shouted back, "Thul! Thul! Thul!" The torch-bearing warrior then came closer. Sezhiyan now understood what was happening. Irungovel and the soldiers left to him must be moving about in this very forest. The torch-bearing warrior must have come to warn them if some other enemy approached. When he called "Enemy", they answered "Thul" to show whether those ahead were enemies or not. If they failed to give that reply, the torch-bearer would ride back and warn the others of danger, allowing them to flee. That, Sezhiyan concluded, must be how it worked.

The torch-bearer, once he came near, looked at the other warriors and asked, "Well? Victory?"

"Here is the sign of victory!" they said, pointing to Sezhiyan.

<!-- source boundary: scan 60 → scan 61 -->

Sezhiyan's eyes were blindfolded. "Why, why are you binding my eyes?" Sezhiyan asked angrily.

"From now on you will not be able to see this world. You saw it a little while ago for the last time; that is the end of your connection with the great sights of the world! Hm... drag him along!" the torch-bearer ordered, then turned his horse.

With his eyes tightly bound, Sezhiyan walked on, stumbling over stones and thorns, blood running from his feet. They all reached the shore of a lake. To the amazement of anyone who might see them, many houses made of timber stood there in the middle of the forest. One wooden house, built with the appearance of a palace, stood impressively. There was also a long barracks suitable for the warriors to stay in. The houses had been hastily built by cutting forest trees and stacking them as whole logs, without even sawing them into proper planks.

Opposite the palace-like house stood another wooden building without the least ventilation. It had only a heavy door, and a large lock hung on it. On the front of the building, painted in white, were the words: "Road to Death."

Behind the palace house stood another building, slightly less grand but still palace-like. Had his eyes not been bound, Sezhiyan would have understood that the king stayed in the front palace, while the women of the royal household, the queen and the princess, stayed in the one behind.

Defeated by Karikala Cholan, Irungovel had built a wooden palace in that forest and was living there with his commanders, ministers, queen and princess, driven by the determination to settle his feud and take revenge somehow. His government, with no people but only warriors, ministers and chief officers, functioned there in the forest. Even there he had no peace. Until Karikalan was brought down to the earth, he would have no rest. He wandered about possessed by that fury. Just as his dream seemed about to ripen, <!-- source boundary: scan 61 → scan 62 -->Sezhiyan stepped in his way. His anger turned upon Sezhiyan. As a result, Irungovel's warriors had brought Sezhiyan there by a stratagem.

The warriors dragged Sezhiyan into the palace-like house. A wooden throne and seats arranged around it for counsellors filled the hall and gave it a strange appearance. There was no one in the hall when Sezhiyan was brought in; only the seats were there. Once everyone had entered, the outer doors were closed. The torch-bearer gently struck a bell hanging in the hall. A small door in one corner opened with a creak. Through it someone came staggering in and raised his face in the torchlight. Though he was an old man, there was no darkness of old age on his face; a cruel darkness had spread across it.

"Who's there?" he cried, opening his mouth wide. How terrible! A voice that pierced the ear! And how sharp were the deep-set eyes of that old man, who looked like a bogeyman used to frighten little children!

"Honourable Minister! My respects. Here, they have brought Sezhiyan!" the torch-bearer said respectfully to the old man.

"Oh! So there is a minister here too!" Sezhiyan concluded to himself.

The aged minister came close to Sezhiyan and looked him up and down. He laughed grotesquely. "Are you the warrior who killed Villavan?" he asked with hatred trembling in his voice. Sezhiyan stood silent.

"Just wait and see how many kinds of torture await you for taking the Chola's side and killing the commander of our Velir clan!" he shouted, holding his breath between the words. There was firmness in that cry. The words came out with force and deliberation. Unable to open his eyes, Sezhiyan spoke.

<!-- source boundary: scan 62 → scan 63 -->

"You are not merely an old man; you have also been a minister. For a man like you to speak whatever comes to his mouth, without even giving age the respect due to it, is not proper. That alone I wish to say in this forest where my eyes are bound." He spoke with dignity, then cried out with energy and emotion, "A man of the Pandiyan land does not fear death!"

"Sezhiyan! Boy! Remember that you are speaking to Minister Senthalaiyar!" The torch-bearer gave him a tap on the head. The instant the blow fell, Sezhiyan became a tiger struck by a spear. He erupted like a volcano. With the very chain that bound him, he flung down the men holding it. Taken by surprise, the warriors gripping his chains scattered and fell like lambs caught in the middle of a forest torrent.

There were shackles on his hands and feet, and his eyes were bound—yet he was ready to fight. He raised his shackled hands to tear off the blindfold. Before he could, one man rushed at Sezhiyan with a sword. Sezhiyan seized that sword and hurled the attacker down. By then many sword-points surrounded him. Watching Sezhiyan fight without sight, guided only by sound, even Minister Senthalaiyar was astonished. Five or six warriors were wounded by his sword and fled. Yet the final victory was not his. Irungovel's men prevailed. Sezhiyan was bound tightly once more.

Then came the sound of horses' hooves. The street door opened. The men who dismounted brought in the body of Commander Villavan. Silence fell over everything. Grief suddenly filled the hall. They laid Villavan's warrior form in the middle of the hall. The minister went near the body and bent over it in the torchlight. His face became still more terrible.

"Enemy! Enemy! Enemy!" he cried, raising his voice step by step.

Those standing around answered angrily with a resounding cry: "Thul! Thul! Thul!"

<!-- source boundary: scan 63 → scan 64 -->

A little silence followed. The minister looked at the horsemen and asked, "Has the king still not arrived?"

"The king came behind us. He has gone to change his clothes!" one warrior replied.

By then, from the small side door through which the minister had appeared, King Irungovel came moving in with the measured sway of a chariot. Though the Velir ruler was only a minor king, his arrival in his great hall would have been a sight to behold. Today, in a dark wooden palace, his troubled heart could not erase the royal gait that would stay with him until death. The sight of him entering touched the hearts of his warriors. It was because they were so moved that they treated their own lives as nothing and endured hardship beside Irungovel, hoping to bring renewal to his fortunes. Irungovel's eyes first fell on Villavan's body. He came close and stood there. Tears poured from his eyes. He looked at the warriors. Everyone, including the minister, stood with heads bowed.

"For me—for the greatness of my life—for us—for our country—let us bow our heads and pay homage to the honoured body of the valiant commander Villavan, who went forth without valuing his own life!"

Irungovel uttered these words in a voice heavy with grief. His voice caught; had he not been a king, he would have broken into sobs. Yet with a speed exactly opposite to the sorrow with which those words had emerged, he suddenly burst out, "Where is the man who killed Villavan?"

"Here!" said the minister.

Irungovel went to Sezhiyan and stood staring at him. It seemed as though he would suck out Sezhiyan's blood with his gaze alone. Almost without realizing it, his hand went to his dagger; he drew it. The aged minister stopped him. Sezhiyan knew nothing of this. Unable to open his eyes, he stood facing forward, absorbed in some thought.

"Take Sezhiyan away and keep him under proper guard!" ordered the aged minister. Thinking there must be some reason in the minister's order, <!-- source boundary: scan 64 → scan 65 -->Irungovel raised no objection. Sezhiyan was dragged away. They decided that Villavan should be buried at daybreak.

The door of the building whose front bore the words "Road to Death!" was opened. Sezhiyan was dragged inside. When the men entered, bats that had been trapped there flew about in confusion. Sezhiyan's hands and feet were chained to a large pillar so that he could not move. Then the door of that dreadful building was shut. Men were posted on guard all around it.
