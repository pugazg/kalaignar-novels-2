5

The Gathering of Siva Devotees

<!-- source boundary: scan 81 → scan 82 -->

The exhausted horse walked on, swaying from side to side. She too sat weary, heaving deep sighs. Thinking over the two events—her meeting with Thamarai and several other women, and her encounter with the Yavana elder—she concluded that Irungovel's residence must be somewhere in that region and that those women too must belong to Irungovel's household. Sezhiyan must have fallen into their hands. The worry over what torments they might be inflicting on him, and the fear of what they might already have done to him by now, left her deeply disturbed.

Thoughts about the future of the great Chola land swirled through the young woman's mind and, without her knowing it, brought a hollow smile to her face. Karikala Peruvalathan was a great warrior with mighty shoulders. His armies possessed a strength no one could overcome. It was the thought that the responsibility of protecting such a powerful man from a conspiracy had been placed upon her that brought that smile to Muthunagai's face.

After a long, long while, she finally reached the very edge of Puhar city.

The horse stepped across the boundary of Maruvurpakkam. Merchants and other skilled workers were busily attending to their occupations. Muthunagai rode along the street, casting her gaze to both sides. Then the cry "Love is Siva! Virtue is Saivism!" reached her ears.

She stopped the horse and watched. A group of ten or fifteen Siva devotees was approaching. Muthunagai was startled. She decided that Irungovel's men were moving about the city to carry out some other <!-- source boundary: scan 82 → scan 83 -->plot, just as they had carried Sezhiyan away.

Muthunagai followed the band of devotees. They went to every house in Maruvurpakkam, raising Saivite cries. They entered some large houses, received things there, and came out looking pleased.

Muthunagai could not contain her anger. A fierce struggle rose within her at the thought of the pure guise worn by traitors; she resolved that, somehow, she would prevent their attempt from succeeding that very day. Her heart ached that the people of the Chola capital were being fooled, unable to see the true nature of the prowling predators who had put on disguises in order to overthrow the country. The devotees passed, looking like men who had renounced everything, between those who carried and sold fragrant powders, vermilion paste, sandalwood and aromatic incense, and those who delighted in buying them; the sight filled her with fury. She could not bear to see people disguised as *kottai-kattigal* moving among sellers of glittering ornaments set with silk, coral, gold, gems and pearls.

She wondered whether she should shout, gather everyone together and have those Siva devotees surrounded. But if she did that, Irungovel's conspiracy might never be exposed. Realizing that it might instead alert him and make him act cautiously, she continued to follow the devotees.

Crossing Maruvurpakkam, the devotees went toward Pattinappakkam. She followed them without letting them out of sight as they passed places such as Vellidai Mandram, Ilanji Mandram, Nadungal Mandram, Bootha Sathukkam and Paavai Mandram. The moment she saw Vellidai Mandram, her eyes filled with tears. She sighed at the thought of that hall's greatness. There anyone could tie up their trade goods in bundles, write the owner's name upon them, leave them there and go about their work; the hall had neither doors nor guards. Even so, not a single bundle would <!-- source boundary: scan 83 → scan 84 -->be stolen. She grieved that traitors had sprung up in a country where justice flourished with such distinction—in a land filled with discipline.

She thought of her father. A curtain of tears fell before her eyes, as though screening the road along which the devotees were walking.

After passing places such as a Buddhist vihara, the devotees entered a Siva temple. The sight greatly surprised Muthunagai. If they were Irungovel's men, would they dare to enter a Siva temple so boldly? She was confused.

She dismounted from the horse, went inside and watched. The truth became clear to her.

None of them was an agent of Irungovel. They were genuine Siva devotees from the city of Puhar. Muthunagai felt ashamed of her mistake. Yet her thoughts darted elsewhere and came back.

She took a palm leaf and hurriedly wrote on it:

"Beloved Siva devotees!

"Greetings. In our capital, some enemies have entered in the guise of devotees and are engaged in treacherous work. To help in identifying them, it would be best if you did not go outside for a few months and remained within the temple itself. Please do not ask who I am. Greetings."

Muthunagai took the palm leaf to the leader of the band of devotees, handed it to him and stood waiting for a reply.

The leader of the devotees was Thiruneettradiyar. He was middle-aged and powerfully built; whenever he looked at anyone he did so only after summoning an expression of grace to his face. The reason was that, by nature, valour and majesty shone more strongly on his face than grace did. He stared intently at Muthunagai.

"Do not let him go! He is the one who has come here to commit some treachery!" Thiruneettradiyar shouted.

<!-- source boundary: scan 84 → scan 85 -->

The other devotees surrounded Muthunagai and seized her. In the confusion her male disguise came apart. The leader of the devotees saw before him a woman radiant with beauty. At the sight of the graceful maiden standing alone—enough to make one wonder, "Was she fashioned from molten gold? Or from the imagination of poets?"—Thiruneettradiyar's heart melted like heated lead, scorched through his ribs, seized his lower belly, and seemed to rush through every blood vessel in his body.

Thiruneettradiyar cast a sidelong glance at the group of disciples standing around him. They disappeared. He caught Muthunagai by the hand and pulled her toward him. Muthunagai raised her arm and dealt him a resounding slap. Her fingers marked his cheek as plainly as the three bands upon his forehead, tearing out lines of blood.

The devotee would endure no more. He sprang at her, clasped her against his body and tried to take her into another part of the temple. Just then, with a cry of "Hey, stop!" a figure struck at the devotee's grip. Muthunagai turned in astonishment. It was none other than the same Yavana elder!

The devotee opposed the elder. The whole band of devotees arrived there. The Yavana elder's sword whirled toward them. Seeing the elder's tiger-like leap, the devotees began running in every direction.

Seeing that this was her chance, Muthunagai straightened and put her male clothing back in order, ran outside, mounted her horse and sped away.

She was eager to know what had happened inside the temple. After going a long distance, she turned back toward the temple. Just as she was approaching it, the Yavana elder came out of the temple, mounted his horse and rode away. She hid safely near a mandapam so that the Yavana elder would not notice her.

After a short while, she rode slowly around the temple and set out toward her home.
