7

The Floundering Lotus

<!-- source boundary: scan 105 → scan 106 -->

Irungovel went inside the palace, changed his clothes, and came to the front of the wooden palace. A horn sounded to announce his arrival. All the warriors came running and stood in formation. The ruler of the Velir clan looked at them majestically and nodded in acknowledgment of the respectful salute they offered him.

"Hm! Bring the prisoners!" ordered a commander.

Four or five warriors ran off.

A bell rang with a terrifying sound.

A hundred warriors came carrying blazing torches. The torches were planted in the ground there. Their flames flared so fiercely that it was impossible to stand even at a distance. Many prisoners, their hands in fetters, were dragged in.

Sezhiyan was one of them. Exhausted, he came along barely able to walk. Yet even in that condition, his valour flew like a banner.

The prisoners were made to stand amid the torches planted in the ground. How could they bear, from so close by, the heat spewed by those demons of flame, their fiery tongues twisting and burning in every direction?

Sezhiyan realized that all the prisoners were men of Chola country. He even grew angry with the Chola king for being unaware of the danger approaching his land while so many people were being carried away from the Chola realm.

<!-- source boundary: scan 106 → scan 107 -->

Why had all these men been imprisoned?

What positions did they hold in the Chola palace? Were they warriors? Ministers? Or leading men of the country?

Who was there to answer Sezhiyan's questions? They too looked eagerly at Sezhiyan.

Every pair of eyes seemed to ask: "Who is the new man?"

Irungovel began to speak. No! No! He began to roar.

"Slaves! How long are you going to suffer like this? In any case, none of you is going to be freed. If you want at least to remain in prison without hardship and torment, tell me the truth plainly. Hey, Sezhiya! Foolish young man! Warrior of Pandiyan country! You too may tell whatever truths you know. They say Karikala Cholan goes to a secret place to think all by himself! Where is that place? Look at these fools—they are suffering mortal agony because they will not tell that truth. Sezhiya! If you know it, tell me and save yourself!"

Sezhiyan replied to Irungovel:

"You yourself call the place where the Chola king goes to think a secret place. If it is secret, how would I or these men know it?"

"What arrogance! Hey! Do you know the law here? If any prisoner from Chola country opens his mouth, he is allowed to open it only to tell the truth about Karikalan. Otherwise, he must not open his mouth here even to cry 'Ah! Ah!' in pain! Understand that."

— Roaring angrily like this, Irungovel left the place. The Chola prisoners and Sezhiyan stood writhing amid the cruel heat of the torches.

A short while after Irungovel left, some of the prisoners fainted from the heat and fell to the ground with one thud after another.

<!-- source boundary: scan 107 → scan 108 -->

Watching that dreadful scene, Sezhiyan stood rumbling like a volcano. He did not sway. He did not move. Though the fire scorched his body, he stood with a look of heroic majesty. From inside the wooden palace, Queen Perunthevi, Thamarai and the others watched that cruel punishment through the windows. Just then King Irungovel entered. Still lying on her bed, the queen stretched out her hand, called him near and received him with affection.

Looking at his sister, Irungovel asked, "What is it, Thamarai? Why are you looking on with such deep sorrow?"

Gathering a little courage, Thamarai said, "Everything else may be as it is, brother! But it seems to me that punishing the Chola prisoners this cruelly crosses the bounds of humaneness!"

"Crazy girl! You too feel pity for those sinners? We have lost our country, lost our well-being, and live like wanderers, leading the life of beasts in the deep forest. Whatever suffering we inflict on those who reduced us to this state is deserved!" Irungovel shouted like a man possessed.

Thamarai did not want to argue any further. She went over to the window and began looking at the prisoners through it. One by one they were falling down. Her eyes filled with tears. She stood there, utterly absorbed in the sight.

The queen began speaking softly to Irungovel, coughing now and then.

"It seems your sister saw a mute young man at the edge of the forest..."

"Is that so?.... Hm... what of it?"

"It seems that young man is wandering about seeking revenge on Karikalan..."

The first question of suspicion arose in Irungovel's mind.

<!-- source boundary: scan 108 → scan 109 -->

"A mute young man!"

"Wandering about to take revenge on Karikalan!"

The queen's words echoed in turn.

"Then what?"

— the king asked eagerly, seeking an answer.

"I do not know whether your sister has developed some affection for him, or what kind of affection it may be. Apparently the young man knows effective methods of treatment. Thamarai told me that if he were brought here, he could treat me and also be of help to you in taking revenge on Karikalan. I told her I wanted none of it and refused."

"Why did you refuse?... What is the young man's name, she says?"

"She said Muthu!"

"'Muthu!' Irungovel's lips moved.

"Perunthevi! If he is a good healer, why did you stop her from bringing him?" Saying this, he called his sister. "Thamarai! Thamarai! Come here! I hear you told Perunthevi something. If he is a good healer, bring that mute young man here. He is a good man, isn't he?" Irungovel asked excitedly.

"Yes, brother! He seems to be a good man. They say there is no herb he does not know. Like us, he too is an enemy of the Chola king!"

— Thamarai said happily.

"A very young man?"

"A young man, yes!"

"A young man whose moustache has begun to grow?"

"I don't know whether it has started growing or not; there is no moustache on his face!"

"Name?"

"Muthu!"

<!-- source boundary: scan 109 → scan 110 -->

"Reddish complexion?... Dark?"

"Reddish! Why are you asking questions like this, brother? Did you already know that young man?"

"Uh-uh—no! No! I asked only out of suspicion. Bring him here and begin the queen's treatment. One more thing—we must be careful about everything. That mute young man must not, by any chance, learn that I am here. Tell him that King Irungovel has run away somewhere. If I come here to see you, I will come only after changing my face!"

"Changing your face? Will you buy someone else's face and put it on?" the queen asked with a laugh, and began to cough.

The king smiled as he embraced her. Embarrassed to watch them, Thamarai looked outside through the window. More than half the prisoners had fainted and fallen down. The others stood writhing. The sight of Sezhiyan alone standing without swaying or moving caught Thamarai's attention.

"What strength of heart that young man has! Even with tongues of fire whirling around him and spewing flame, there is not the slightest shadow of fear in his eyes. What majesty in the way he stands like a stone pillar! Who could conquer a land filled with warriors like this? True Tamils would not bargain away their valour even if chisels were driven into their eyes. Though my brother knows that, I do not understand why he is inflicting these cruel punishments."

— Thinking this to herself, Thamarai grew troubled. As she watched, almost all the prisoners fainted and fell. Sezhiyan alone remained standing. Even as he stood, his head began to spin. Without his realizing it, his eyes kept closing and opening. Thamarai expected that he too would collapse very soon. An excitement she herself did not understand made her heart pound. She stood gazing only at him with an inexpressible eagerness.

<!-- source boundary: scan 110 → scan 111 -->

Irungovel walked straight up to him and stood staring at him. The determination that shone as though written across the brave young man's face touched Irungovel's heart. He looked at the nearby warriors and ordered them to extinguish the torches. Within a few moments all the torches were put out. Sezhiyan, who had been on the point of falling, steadied himself, opened his eyes wide and looked ahead. Irungovel stood before him with a smile. Sezhiyan looked around. The sight of the Chola prisoners lying unconscious filled him with fury.

Looking at Irungovel, he shouted, "Are you a warrior too?"

"Why? Do you have doubts about it?" Irungovel asked calmly.

"A man with no compassion in his heart is not called a warrior—he is called a brute; a savage. There are worse names than these. But there is no place for such names in the beautiful Tamil dictionary!"

"How did you discover that I have no compassion in my heart?"

"Here—ask these extinguished torches! They will give you the answer!"

"Young man! It is not the torches that were extinguished!.. The life of my country was extinguished! The undiminished great glory of the Velir clan was extinguished! By whom? By whom, brother, came this devastation? By that tyrant Karikala Peruvalathan whom you are trying to protect!"

"Ah! Karikalan, a tyrant? Mother Kaveri sings his praises on seeing the skill of his rule! Seeing his compassionate heart, Poompuhar itself plucks the veena of praise! Countries beyond the seas acclaim him as 'Karikalan, Thirumavalavan, the great king of the South.' And you say he is a tyrant! This is the first time I have heard such a marvel—an explanation that calls white black!"

"Who else but a tyrant would seize my sacred country?"

"Your country lost in war—why will you not accept it?"

<!-- source boundary: scan 111 → scan 112 -->

"Young man! What is there for me to discuss with you? I will not rest until I have taken revenge on that Chola king, who imagines that he has enslaved me and my people, and on that Pandiyan who stands with him!"

"Go to ruin for all I care! What sentence have you given me? Tell me quickly!"

At those words, the anger of Irungovel, who had been patient until then, suddenly surged. He had thought that allowing Sezhiyan to speak might serve some purpose, but he was not merely disappointed; the young man's replies made him erupt like a volcano. He ground his teeth.

"Sentence? You arrogant wretch! Sentence? I have planned to kill you little by little! I will take out your eyes, hold them in my hand, and delight in showing those very eyes the sight of your limbs being torn apart little by little—piece by piece! Is that sentence enough? Would you like some new method as well?"

Irungovel snarled like a tiger! Sezhiyan began to laugh aloud.

Still laughing, he mocked him: "You are shattering, little by little, my belief that the king of the Velir clan is a great warrior!"

Irungovel expected his laughter to die down. But Sezhiyan kept looking at Irungovel and laughing. Taking that laughter as a great humiliation, Irungovel sprang forward and began slapping Sezhiyan across the cheeks—smack, smack. Sezhiyan stood there and took the blows. His hands were fettered so that he could not resist. There were iron chains on his legs as well! Irungovel struck Sezhiyan's cheeks alternately until his own hands began to ache. Then he stopped of his own accord and left the place.

Thamarai had watched these terrible scenes without blinking. As soon as her brother left, she came out slowly, called one of the warriors, and ordered him to revive the prisoners lying <!-- source boundary: scan 112 → scan 113 --> unconscious and give them something to eat. Obeying Thamarai's order, he gathered a few other warriors and began tending to all the prisoners who had fainted.

Then she slowly approached the place where Sezhiyan stood. Without looking at her, he stood with his eyes closed and his face lifted toward the sky. Thamarai thought she should help him somehow. She decided for herself that helping him in that way would not be disloyal to her brother. Though she did not believe the prisoners should be released, she could not bear to see them tortured.

"Do you want some water?"

Hearing a woman's voice, Sezhiyan opened his eyes and looked. He could not believe his own eyes. Who was this woman standing before him? Where had he seen her before...?

A warrior came to Sezhiyan saying that Peruvazhuthi Pandiyan was calling him; Sezhiyan immediately set out. Inside the palace, in a separate mansion, the Pandiyan was pacing in deep thought. When he saw Sezhiyan, his face brightened and he received him warmly.

"Do you know why I called you?"

"I don't know, my king."

"A wedding is going to take place, Sezhiya!"

"For whom? When?"

"For you, Sezhiya! I have found the girl. Here, look at the bride's portrait!"

The Pandiyan chose one portrait from the seven or eight there and showed it to Sezhiyan. Sezhiyan looked at it. He found it difficult to tear his eyes at once from the image of the beautiful woman who appeared there, smiling in full radiance.

"Who is this woman? What place is she from?"

<!-- source boundary: scan 113 → scan 114 -->

"Do not ask all that now! Do you like the girl or not? Tell me only that!"

...From Sezhiyan's embarrassment, the Pandiyan understood that he agreed.

For many days after that incident, Peruvazhuthi never again raised the subject of Sezhiyan's marriage. Sezhiyan had still not finished searching for where the beauty in the portrait—the one who had unsettled his heart—might be. So many events had taken place in the meantime! Yet none of them had erased from his heart the memory of that painted maiden who had brought about a new change within him.

At times he had been tormented by the doubt: 'Does such a woman truly exist in the world? Or is she an imagined beauty brought to life by the painter's artistry?'

Now that vain doubt had disappeared. He had seen the woman from the painting alive. Where? In the enemy's house! In what condition? At a time when he himself stood suffering with fetters on his hands. What connection did she have with Irungovel? What relation? His questions needed immediate answers. But first he decided to answer her question.

"I want water!" he said.

She called a warrior and told him to give Sezhiyan water at once. The warrior brought water and poured it into Sezhiyan's mouth. After drinking it, he cleared his throat and regained a little strength. She had not failed to notice the swelling on Sezhiyan's cheek caused by her brother's rough hands. Sezhiyan thanked the man who gave him water. He replied, "It's all right."

"You're from this place too, aren't you?"

At Sezhiyan's question, the man blinked, not knowing what answer to give.

If he said "yes," Sezhiyan would place him too among the list of tormentors; if he said "no," would Sezhiyan believe him or not? Thamarai could not bring herself to answer.

<!-- source boundary: scan 114 → scan 115 -->

"Won't you tell me?" he asked with urgency.

"Why do you need all these questions?" Thamarai said, and swiftly left the place.

Sezhiyan, having received no proper answer, stood bewildered and watched her go. One by one, the prisoners who had fainted were dragged away and shut inside timber sheds. At last Sezhiyan too was dragged away, thrown into a prison marked 'Way to Death,' and the guard outside was strengthened.

After giving Sezhiyan her reply and moving away, Thamarai stood at a distance and watched anxiously as he was taken away. She watched until he was locked up, then wandered here and there outside the palace.

At that moment, Muthu entered the chamber of her thoughts. She had never expected her brother's permission to bring Muthu home to come so easily. Not wanting to let the opportunity slip away, she began planning to go at once, meet Muthu and bring him back.

Muthunagai had decided to wait by the waterfall until Thamarai came to take her away, and she remained there through the night. The sky had drawn a black curtain without even letting the stars show themselves. From time to time there was thunder too, like the cough of a man with a cold! With flashes of lightning like sparks rising in the great hall of the world, nature seemed to paint the picture of her heart.

It seemed to her that the poet Karikannanar stood before her in tears, saying, 'What suffering I endure because you are away from me, child—why, my daughter, do you torture me like this?' At once an answer would rise in her own heart.

'What if it is my father who betrays the motherland? What if it is anyone at all? To stand against them is the duty of those who truly love their country!' she told herself.

<!-- source boundary: scan 115 → scan 116 -->

Is it not strange that the poor girl who had left home after concluding that Irungovel, in the guise of the Yavana elder, was friendly with her father was now, without even realizing it, floundering after surrendering her own heart to that same Irungovel?

The sense of duty she still had to fulfil kept pursuing her from another side; poor Thamarai, believing her to be a man, was falling at her feet saying she had become a slave to love. With one thought overtaking another in the whirling storm inside her mind, the flowering creeper sat leaning against the tree with her eyes gently closed. The frightening thunder and lightning disturbed the calm of a few birds perched on the branches. They cried out and moved about from branch to branch. Muthunagai listened closely to their calls. She too felt like speaking something aloud. Keeping her mouth shut like a mute was painful to her. If she were at home, she would be talking about something with the poet. The poet too took a special pleasure in answering her questions! With the doubts she raised about each poem he wrote and the explanations the poet gave in reply, the whole house would look like a Tamil garden. For someone like her to keep her mouth closed...?

The cries of the birds only increased her urge! As though answering them, she cried out just as they did. Realizing that she could alter her voice and call like the birds filled her with delight! She listened carefully to the sounds of different birds and competed with them in calling out. Hearing her cries, even the birds that had been asleep woke and began to call. She found it immensely amusing. She decided she could spend the night this way.

Hearing all this noise, the horse too began to neigh. She went over, patted it, and then returned to sit beneath the tree. A little of the parrot's alarm call! She made the same sound. The dry cry of the owl? She won at that too. Then came the 'ngoy' sound of the garuda, like the sound of an arrow leaving the bow <!-- source boundary: scan 116 → scan 117 -->—she won that contest as well. Her thoughts began to work intensely. For a while, silence; she tried changing her voice in every possible way and making sounds. The contest with the birds had ended.

Her urge to practise speaking in a man's voice grew stronger. If she went with Thamarai, she could not keep writing and showing messages there too, she thought. If she could call like birds, could she not learn to speak like men? Courage arose in her. She began to speak. She did not succeed at once. But confidence dawned that she would succeed. She had memorized many of the poems written by her father. She began declaiming all of them in a man's voice. In the end, she succeeded!

Now she could speak like a man. But her throat hurt a little. What could be done about that? She herself had taken on the responsibility of fulfilling this duty and set out! Suddenly a doubt came to Muthunagai: did her natural woman's voice still remain or not? She tried speaking in her own voice. For a moment she was at a loss—what should she say? Then she spoke.

"The moment I saw you in that ruined mandapam, I lost my heart to you!"

Only after speaking did she realize what she had said! She had entered the test only to see whether her own voice remained—why had she wandered into the grove of love? Did that mean she was in love with that woodcutter?

She felt shy at the thought of herself. It seemed as though a hand had come to rest on her shoulder! The sensation did not make her start up in alarm. She remained as she was. The hand seemed to press and hold her shoulder. She made no attempt to free herself. Instead, she leaned back into the hand. Something strange came over her. It was a dizziness like being held in the embrace of a powerful arm. She closed her eyes lightly and savoured the sensation. Then her hand moved toward her shoulder. As though to push away the rough hand gripping her there, her soft hand crept upward, caressing and enjoying the feeling. It rose toward her shoulder.
