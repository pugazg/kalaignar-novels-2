8

The Disguise Falls Away!

<!-- source boundary: scan 119 → scan 120 -->

Tch! Tch! What foolishness! Until now it had not been a man's arm embracing her shoulder, but the arm of a tree! The base of a fallen branch from the tree against which she had been leaning had struck her shoulder, given her a moment's bliss of love, and deceived her.

Muthunagai covered her face as though someone in the darkness were watching her from in front. The thought of the woodcutter would not leave her alone. She decided that if she met him, she must tell him, "Cut any tree in this forest you like—but do not cut this one."

"Tak! Tak!" A sound came at intervals. She walked slowly toward it. It was the sound of wood being chopped! Who would come to cut a tree at this hour in the pitch-dark forest? Could it perhaps be him? Suspicious and curious, she went forward.

As she went on, the sound drew nearer. She could also see the glow of a lamp in the direction from which it came.

Keeping herself hidden, she moved toward the place where the light could be seen.

A torch was burning. Someone was chopping at a tree beside it.

The man doing the cutting had his back turned, so she could not identify him.

His build looked very much like that of the man she had met at the pavilion. But she could not be certain from that examination. Muthunagai stood bewildered, not knowing whether to go closer or remain there and watch.

"Who could it be?" The thought was still running through her mind when, with a cracking rush, the tree broke and began to fall <!-- source boundary: scan 120 → scan 121 -->down. The terrifying crash of the falling tree echoed through the entire forest. Muthunagai shuddered. Did the felled tree too have to fall toward her? Seeing that it was going to fall on her, Muthunagai cried, "Oh!" and collapsed to the ground.

Hearing a woman's voice, the woodcutter ran over with the torch. Fortunately, the tree had fallen very close to her without striking her. Had it shifted even a little, Muthunagai's body would have been crushed. Planting the torch in the ground, the woodcutter lifted her. She opened her eyes fearfully and looked.

"Muthu! Muthu! What are you doing here at this hour?" The moment she heard those words, everything became clear. The very man she had come hoping to find stood before her.

She had run there with an uncontrollable longing that it might indeed be him; now she was afraid, thinking, "Why did I have to find him?"

"Why are you cutting wood at this hour?" Muthunagai asked, looking at him—not in a woman's voice, but in the man's voice she had newly learned.

Irungovel, standing there as a woodcutter, was astonished to hear her speak in a man's voice. Without showing his surprise, he looked at her and asked, "You said you would not speak until your goal was achieved. Yet you have spoken now!"

"My goal is nearing fulfilment. I am certain now that victory will be mine somehow. So I have begun speaking boldly!" Muthunagai replied.

"What is your goal? Is its whole world contained within this forest?" Irungovel asked.

"Yes," she said.

Irungovel did not want to probe further. Concluding that Thamarai would in any case bring her to the wooden palace and that he could then learn the truth about her little by little, he turned the conversation elsewhere.

<!-- source boundary: scan 121 → scan 122 -->

"Good thing the broken tree did not fall on you. What brought you here, man?"

After remaining silent for a little while, Muthunagai said, "I was sleeping in that pavilion over there. I heard someone cutting wood. I thought perhaps it might be you, so I came. Tell me—do you practise your trade only at night? Are you stealing timber?"

Enjoying the clever way she spoke in a man's voice, Irungovel nodded and said, "Yes!" How could she know that he had come only because he longed to see her again? If Thamarai took her home, he had feared that he might not get another chance to meet her, and so he had rushed there to see her once before that happened. He knew very well that she had been sitting by the waterfall. Yet he pretended to believe her lie that she had been sleeping in the pavilion! He had come to the place where he was cutting wood while watching her at the waterfall. He had even pretended to chop the tree merely to attract her attention. The tree fell. She came—and fell into his trap. Irungovel had not come that night intending to leave without speaking to her. He had come trembling with eagerness to reveal that he knew she was a woman and to pass the night in sweet conversation and pleasure. Yet when she stood before him, he could not summon that courage.

"I'll split the tree into firewood tomorrow. Let us sleep for a while. Come, man, let's go to the pavilion!" he called to Muthunagai.

"No, I'm not coming!" she said.

"Oh, come on, man!" Irungovel said, catching her hand and pulling her.

"Uh-uh... I have work to do."

"What work is there at night? You can see to everything in the morning. Rain is coming too. Come on, man, let's go!" Irungovel said, pulling her again,

<!-- source boundary: scan 122 → scan 123 -->

and, as though to prove him right, a drizzle began to fall. The thunder too grew loud.

"See? The rain's already here! Come on, man!" he urged, picking up the torch and pulling her along. Unable to say anything, she went with him. What else could she do? She wanted to go, yet she was afraid of what might happen if she did. Still, she went with him. They entered the pavilion just as the rain began to pour heavily. She grew worried about the horse tied near the waterfall.

"What are you thinking about, man?" Irungovel asked, giving her a pat on the back.

"Nothing!" she managed. Looking at him, she asked, "You work so hard cutting wood day and night—what do you get from it?"

Keeping the torch burning brightly, he replied, "Do you think one can fill one's stomach without hardship?"

"Are you married?" Muthunagai asked.

She had asked the question too hastily. Before she had even finished it, her body began to tremble.

"Not yet. What marriage is there for a lump like me, man? But tell me—are you married? How many children? Is your wife as beautiful as you?" Irungovel rattled off the questions one after another.

"Uh-uh... I'm not married," Muthunagai said.

"Then shall the two of us get married?" Irungovel asked mischievously.

"What! The two of us?" Muthunagai exclaimed as waves of amazement and alarm swept over her—along with a secret thrill within.

"Tch... tch... two men marrying each other! I meant, you marry a woman and I marry a woman. That's what I asked, man!" Irungovel said, covering his joke.

<!-- source boundary: scan 123 → scan 124 -->

Muthunagai laughed.

"All right, let's sleep!" Irungovel told her.

Thinking that it would be dangerous to keep talking, Muthunagai agreed that they should sleep and lay down on one side of the pavilion. The feelings rising within her frightened her greatly. She trembled at the thought that something untoward might happen. She planned to pretend to sleep and run away once he fell asleep. But if she was so afraid, why had she come at all? Does desire know shame? He too lay down very near her. How could she stop him? If she did, she would have to reveal the truth that she was a woman. Holding her breath, saying nothing, she began pretending to sleep. And would Irungovel be able to sleep?

"...Tch, tch, tch! This light is ruining my sleep!" he muttered. He got up and, without putting out the torch, carried it behind a broken wall in the pavilion. Then he came back and was about to lie down near her.

"Lie a little farther away! Otherwise I won't be able to sleep!" she said.

"All right! All right! Sleep!" he said, moving a little farther away.

Both of them lay pretending to sleep. A little time passed this way. Muthunagai felt something crawling near her feet. She lay utterly still. She thought it must be one of his toes. Her heart went thump, thump. The thing crawling over her foot felt smooth and slippery.

"Ayyo! Snake! Snake!" she screamed, rushing to Irungovel and clinging tightly to him.

"Where? Where?" he kept asking, but the thought of running to fetch the torch did not occur to him at all. How could it, when she was embracing him? Only after she released him did he take the torch and look all around. The very snake that he had brought there with the medicinal plant <!-- source boundary: scan 124 → scan 125 -->was crawling away. He ran quickly, caught it by the tail, whirled it around and around, and struck it against a pillar of the pavilion. The snake fell dead.

"Hm! Great hero of heroes! Lie down without fear, man!" he said, patting her.

"Uh-uh, I'm frightened. I'm leaving," Muthunagai said.

"Here, I'll leave the light right here. Sleep without fear," he said, setting the torch there.

He too lay down and closed his eyes. Rain was falling outside. As Muthunagai lay with her eyes shut, pretending to sleep, his hand touched her head-cloth. She gave a start. She had been lying there with sweetness rising in her heart as she kept remembering how, frightened by the snake, she had embraced him. Now came another predicament!

Catching the hand that had touched her head-cloth, she demanded, "What's this?"

"Oh, it's terribly cold, man! Give me that head-cloth. I'll wrap it around my body," Irungovel said, and despite her efforts to stop him, he began untying her head-cloth.

Muthunagai could prevent him no longer. Had she utterly hated him, such a situation would never have arisen. She would have turned her face away and spoken sharply at their very first meeting before leaving. But now she was caught between two sides. The loosened head-cloth was in his hand. Thick hair, curling in waves, hung from her head. Though he had known this already, he stood motionless staring at her and let out an astonished "Ah?" as though he had discovered it only now.

She too understood that she could no longer continue the pretence. However firmly a creeper is trained up an iron rod, will it not droop, twist and spread rather than stand stiffly displaying manly strength?

<!-- source boundary: scan 125 → scan 126 -->

Irungovel carried his acting to its climax. "What a wonder! Am I dreaming? Or is some magic taking place before my eyes? Muthu! Aren't you a man? Are you a woman?" he asked in agitation, pretending to be utterly confused.

Muthunagai did not speak. She lowered her head.

Irungovel went near, took her cheeks between his hands, and turned her face toward his. Their faces drew close together. Two pairs of eyes locked onto each other. The tips of their noses were only three fingers apart! The nearby torchlight fell on her face, making it gleam like polished gold. Holding her cheeks in his hands as though he were lifting a blooming lotus in both palms and drinking in its beauty with his eyes, Irungovel kept gazing at her face.

Muthunagai forgot the whole world. Freeing her face from his hands, she leaned against his broad chest. The thunder and rain grew many times fiercer than before. A sudden great gust whirled through and extinguished the torch. Both of them silently blessed nature!

Dawn began to break without caring in the least about those blessings. The rain had stopped completely. A gentle breeze was blowing. The two sat silently watching the sky grow pale.

Irungovel was the one who began to speak. Trying to speak after a long while, he felt some emotion press heavily on his heart. He looked intently at her. Her eyes were moist.

"Why do you look so troubled?" he asked, moving closer. There was no answer.

"Forgive me!" he pleaded. Muthunagai looked at him with compassion.

"Won't you forgive me?" he asked again, shaking her shoulder gently.

"You haven't done anything wrong. Wasn't I the cause of everything?" she said in a trembling voice.

<!-- source boundary: scan 126 → scan 127 -->

"No, I too am to blame. I knew from the very beginning that you were a woman, and that is why I became this close to you," Irungovel said.

"How did you know?"

"When you were bathing in the pavilion pool..."

— He did not finish. She covered her face in shame.

"Leave that aside—what is your real name?"

"Muthunagai!"

"Ah, what a fitting name! Why are you wandering through the forest in these clothes?"

"I told you before, didn't I? I have an important goal!"

"Won't you tell me in detail what that goal is?"

"Why not tell you after I understand who you are? I am certainly going to tell you."

"I'm just an ordinary woodcutter! That's all!"

"You can't deceive me. I have proof of who you are!"

"Proof?" Irungovel asked with a start.

"Here! You went to bring a medicinal leaf for the swelling on my finger. At that time, you accidentally left this palm-leaf behind in the pavilion!" she said, taking out a palm-leaf scroll and holding it out to him.

Irungovel looked at it in surprise, recovered himself, and asked as though he knew nothing, "What does this palm-leaf have to do with me?"

The moment he saw the scroll he had dropped, Irungovel realized that Muthunagai had understood who he was. Yet, pretending ignorance, he asked, "What does this palm-leaf <!-- source boundary: scan 127 → scan 128 -->have to do with me?" She replied, "What does it have to do with you? Here, I'll read it!" and unrolled the leaf.

"Sender: Peruvazhuthi Pandiyar.

Recipient: Veerapandi, spy of Pandiyan country.

Spy!

We have read your palm-leaf and understood its explanation. If it is true that Irungovel is in the forest you mention, collect every detail about it. Find out his location—his circumstances—the number of soldiers he has—and return with all of it. May the effort succeed!

— Peruvazhuthi Pandiyan"

After reading the palm-leaf, Muthunagai asked, "Do you understand now what it has to do with you?"

Irungovel smiled.

How could Muthunagai know that it was a false letter—a forged message—deliberately prepared by Irungovel to deceive his enemies? Realizing that she had believed it completely, Irungovel said, "Good thing it fell into your hands! Give it here!" and took the palm-leaf from her. It was true that Irungovel had deceived her. Yet he could no longer regard her as an enemy. He had fallen for her beauty. He was delighted to know that she too loved him wholeheartedly.

"Hm! Tell me your goal!" he said, embracing her.

"Do you know that I am from Chola country?"

"I know only because you've told me now. Your face gave me the first message that you are a peacock of the South. I bow before your beauty!"

"No! You have made me your slave!"

"Pandiyan country and Chola country are friends; neither has enslaved the other. It is the same with us!"

<!-- source boundary: scan 128 → scan 129 -->

After receiving without delay the reward due for this declaration of love, he asked again, "What is your goal?"

"Whatever your goal is, that is mine too! Irungovel's arrogance must be curbed; certain traitors roaming Chola country in support of him must be taught some sense; and Sezhiyan must be rescued without any harm to his life.."

Irungovel let out a hollow laugh and listened carefully as she spoke.

"Are you going to take any interest in rescuing Sezhiyan safely or not?" Muthunagai asked.

"Everything is contained in Irungovel's downfall!" Irungovel said.

"Even so, Sezhiyan must be rescued without harm. I consider that my first task."

"Muthunagai! Why are you so concerned about Sezhiyan?" he asked, waiting for her answer.

"After he fought Irungovel's commander and won, we treated the wounds on his body at our house. Isn't it a great responsibility to protect people like him who are devoted to their country? Sezhiyan is a very good man, a true Tamil. If not for him, Karikalan would have lost his life exactly as that wretch Irungovel intended!" Muthunagai replied.

Her words burned with zeal for her cause. Irungovel enjoyed it. Yet he found it somewhat difficult to bear being abused to his own face.

"You should not have come for such dangerous work, Muthunagai! Even now it is not too late. I will take care of your task along with mine. You can stay peacefully at home," he said.

<!-- source boundary: scan 129 → scan 130 -->

"Ayyo! Just when the time is ripening and the work is about to succeed, you're trying to stop me! I am going to enter Irungovel's very fort," Muthunagai said enthusiastically.

"How can you do that?" Irungovel asked.

"That brute Irungovel has a foolish sister named Thamarai. She is beautiful, though! The lotus that ought to bloom at the sight of the sun has bloomed at the moon instead and been deceived!"

"I don't understand."

"She believed my disguise, took me for a man, and has begun to love me."

"Hm. And then?"

"What else? I have cleverly played along with her and made a plan to enter Irungovel's palace with her own help. She will definitely come this morning!"

"Oh-ho! You really are clever. Hm... nature helps you! If you dress as a man, people believe you. If I dressed as a woman, who would believe me?" he said with a laugh. She laughed with him. Between their laughter, they exchanged a kiss and lost themselves in it.

"It's getting late. Thamarai will come. I'm leaving!" Muthunagai said, getting up to go.

"Be careful. Irungovel is a very cruel man. If you fall into his hands, that wretch would not hesitate to behave improperly with you!" he said.

"Him? That villain? I will not give him even the chance to touch my corpse. Don't worry about me. You take care of yourself. I'm going."

"Again?"

"Shall we meet here again?"

"When?"

<!-- source boundary: scan 130 → scan 131 -->

"You tell me."

"On the day of the coming full moon!"

"I will certainly come. You must not fail to be here."

"Would I fail? That day I am going to meet two moons in this very place!"
