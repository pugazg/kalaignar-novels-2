1

Karikala Cholan and  
Peruvazhuthi Pandiyan

<!-- source boundary: scan 29 → scan 30 -->

One day at the beginning of the year 20 B.C., Poompuhar, the capital city of the great Chola king Karikalan, shone with a new splendour. On all the sky-reaching mansions that stood along the seashore in Maruvurpakkam, the Chola flag and the Pandiyan flag flew embracing one another. Beautiful festoons had been erected at the mansions of the Yavana notables. Throughout Pattinappakkam, imposing entrance arches set with pearls formed the words, "Long live the friendship of the Cholas and Pandyas!"

The merchants of Maruvurpakkam, the weavers of silk cloth, cotton cloth and woollen cloth, goldsmiths, flower-garland makers, painters, sculptors and sailors all dressed in new clothes and hurried toward the royal street. Landholders, physicians, Vedic scholars, palace officials, bards, musicians and learned poets had dressed Pattinappakkam itself in festival array and, from their respective homes, watched the royal street eagerly without blinking. Everyone rejoiced at the proud sight atop the lighthouse of the port city of Puhar, which seemed to proclaim that even if the whole world became a wilderness of poverty, Poompuhar would never wear the garb of poverty: the fish flag flew there together with the tiger. The Patti Mandapam presented to Karikala Peruvallathan by the king of Magadha shone with light. The victory pavilion given by the ruler of Vachira stood as a summit of beauty. The 'Torana Gate' given by the king of Avanti seemed to cry, "Welcome, conqueror of Madurai! Long live!"

With beautiful women seated on elephants proceeding in front, a great procession unlike any Poompuhar had ever seen moved through that city. Commanders and the soldiers of the fourfold army came in formation, while upon two elephants the two great kings—

<!-- source boundary: scan 30 → scan 31 -->

sat enthroned; seeing that day's procession, the Tamil land itself stood awake in overflowing astonishment.

On one elephant sat the Chola emperor Karikalan. On another elephant, walking slowly beside it, sat Peruvazhuthi, king of the Pandiyan country.

"Long live King Karikalan!"

"Long live Peruvazhuthi Pandiyan!"

"May the friendship of Chola and Pandiyan flourish!"—the cry rose, making the sky resound.

The young men and women of Poompuhar celebrated by scooping up and splashing the waters of the Kaveri. They scattered flowers along every street. "May honey-sweet Tamil triumph!" they cried. Wherever one turned, there was a festival scene! A roar of music! The *agamuzhavu* sounded everywhere. The skin instruments called *idakkai*, *udukkai*, *perikai* and *padagam* all thundered forth. Wind instruments called *kuzhal* and *vangiyam*, and stringed instruments such as the *sakodayazh*, *periyazh* and *makarayazh*, poured enthusiasm into the procession. Smiling as they received the people's greetings and smiling at one another, Karikalan and Peruvazhuthi sat majestically upon their elephants.

The commanders of the Chola country rejoiced again and again at the splendour of that procession as it moved beautifully through the royal streets with chariots, horses, men and a great army in formation.

Seeing the appearance of Pandiyan Peruvazhuthi, the Chola people realized that they were indeed bound to show him respect. With a commanding build and a sharp gaze, wearing across his broad chest garlands strung with Korkai pearls, Peruvazhuthi came in procession with such bearing that the Chola people thought and praised him as a ruler worthy of preserving the honour of the jeweled crown.

"Are those not the shoulders that struck and shook the Himalaya! Is that not the spear in his hand that pierced all the kings of kings who came against him! Is it not the warrior-clan nature that protects the land which shines upon his face! Are we not greatly blessed to have him as our leader—

<!-- source boundary: scan 31 → scan 32 -->

!" Thus all who saw King Karikalan in the procession praised him and rejoiced.

The old blessed him. The young thundered, "Long live our country!"

Adding beauty to the royal streets, the procession of those kings moved slowly, slowly onward!

The eyes of the people of the capital, which were fixed on the two great rulers, did not fail to notice a warrior of the Pandiyan country who rode in the procession on a white horse beside the Pandiyan king, wearing a victorious smile. There was a beauty all its own in the way he sat upon the horse. The very manner in which he held the reins in his hand captivated every heart. A small young curled moustache; hair curling and curling like dried chillies! Every movement seemed to leap with valour! A special charm in his smile! Looking alternately at the kings on the elephants and at that young man on horseback, the ocean of people moved forward with enthusiasm.

The procession approached the streets of the poets in Pattinappakkam. The guardians who nurtured Tamil garlanded the kings and rejoiced. King Karikalan looked eagerly toward the gathering of poets.

The Tamil scholars understood that his sword-like eyes were searching that group with great concern for someone, and asked, "What is it, O king?"

Karikalan asked, "Where is Karikannanar?"

"He is waiting at the entrance of his house," the poets replied.

Karikannanar stood at the entrance of his house. Seeing the delight in his heart burst forth upon his face, Karikalan looked at him with gratitude. The poet's eyes had become pools. He offered the Chola and Pandiyan kings the two flowers that had been set aside amid those tears of joy. The kings received them and placed them upon their jeweled crowns. The Pandiyan saw that, more than the love and respect he showed all poets—

<!-- source boundary: scan 32 → scan 33 -->

Karikalan showed Karikannanar an even greater measure of love and respect; pleased, he removed a pearl necklace from his own neck and placed it upon the poet.

"Poet! Once we reach the palace, I shall send a man for you. Come and bless us in Tamil!" requested the noble ruler of the Chola country.

"I shall come, O king! Long live!" the poet said, offering his respects.

At that moment, the eyes of Pandiyan Peruvazhuthi, seated upon the elephant, darted into Karikannanar's house. At the same time, the eyes of the young warrior seated on horseback near the Pandiyan also sprang toward the house. Hiding beside the edge of the poet's doorway, a beautiful woman stood with only her full-moon face peeping out. The moment she realized that others had seen her, she immediately drew herself inside. The Pandiyan king's gaze hesitated a little before returning from that doorway. With the fear that Karikalan or the others might be watching him, Peruvazhuthi looked around on every side. "Tch! What is this?" he rebuked himself. The young man on horseback looked back and forth between Karikannanar and his house and then merged himself again into the procession.

That lovely face vanished as though a risen moon had broken loose and fallen away! Karikannanar stood there, enjoying the splendour of the procession until it left that street, and only then went into his house.

As he entered, he called with affection in his voice, "Muthunagai! Muthunagai!"

"Father! What do you want?" she asked as she ran to him.

Yes! It was she! That young maiden who had hidden herself and watched the kings in procession!

Seeing her, the poet said, "Did you see, Muthunagai? A pearl ornament for you!" He took the pearl necklace that the Pandiyan had placed around his neck <!-- verified Tamil split-word boundary: scan 33 → scan 34 --> and gave it into her hand. She received it eagerly.

"Pearls from the Pandiyan country have a radiance all their own, don't they, Father?" Muthunagai asked.

"Yes, child! What doubt can there be? Nowhere else in the world are there pearls equal to the pearls of Korkai! About ten years ago, the beautiful Cleopatra, who ruled Egypt and then died so pitifully—it is said that without these pearls she could not even drink liquor. The sound these pearls made in wine cups was supposedly her favourite music! The people of Romapuri are mad for pearls from the Pandiyan country! These pearls shine on every one of their ornaments and garments!"

"Father! The friendship agreement being made today between the Pandiyan king and the Chola king will succeed, won't it?"

"Why do you ask like that? I am the one who has been urging King Karikala Thirumavalavan all these days that such a relationship should be formed! I have confidence that the Chola-Pandiyan friendship will endure!"

"Didn't you say that some petty rulers were trying somehow to break this relationship?"

"That will no longer be possible! This relationship has been firmly established! The petty ruler Irungovel, who was defeated and driven away by our Chola king, is the only one who might try to create some obstacle. There is no need for anyone to be afraid of that."

Even as Karikannanar was saying this to his daughter, a great tumult was heard in the street. The poet started in alarm.

Muthunagai ran toward the doorway! Having run there, she cried, "Father! Father! Come here!"
